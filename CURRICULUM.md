# Curriculum Map — High School Math

This map is the source of truth for coverage, IDs, and progression. Content agents must use
these exact IDs and subject groupings. It is a sensible progression, not a claim of alignment
with any official curriculum.

## Progression principles

- Grades 7–8 build number fluency, first algebra, informal geometry, and data literacy.
- Grade 9 formalizes algebra (radicals, factoring, systems), similarity, coordinate basics,
  and solid measurement.
- Grade 10 deepens functions, proof, transformations, right-triangle trigonometry, and
  introductory statistics.
- Grade 11 adds polynomial/exponential/logarithmic functions, sequences, analytic geometry,
  vectors and matrices, and conditional probability.
- Grade 12 is calculus and discrete/linear mathematics: limits, differentiation, integration,
  complex numbers, combinatorics, 3D vectors, distributions.
- Revisits must change treatment. Do not repeat a topic verbatim across grades.

## Formula ID prefixes

Use `g<grade>-<short-topic>-<name>`, for example:

- `g8-pythagoras-main`
- `g10-quadratic-formula`
- `g12-diff-power-rule`

Exercise IDs: `g<grade>-<short-topic>-ex-<n>`.

## Related-topic policy

- Link 2–4 related topics; prefer the same grade, then immediately earlier/later.
- Only use IDs that exist in this map. Never invent topic slugs.
- Prerequisites are human-readable labels or existing topic IDs; if unsure, use labels.

## Writing expectations by grade

- Grade 7: concrete, visual, short sentences; avoid abstract notation without examples.
- Grade 8: introduce structure and reasoning; more multi-step work.
- Grade 9: formal methods, careful conditions, coordinate reasoning.
- Grade 10: functions, proof, trigonometric modelling.
- Grade 11: abstraction, multi-representation, analytic geometry.
- Grade 12: rigour, calculus reasoning, and combined techniques.
